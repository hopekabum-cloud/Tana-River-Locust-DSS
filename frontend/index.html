How the risk maps are made

Random Forest and XGBoost each learn the environmental conditions at desert locust records compared with background points. Their scores are combined into an ensemble score for every 500 m cell, and the score is grouped into five relative classes.

About the system

Research project. Development of a GIS-Based Decision Support System for Desert Locust Risk Prediction and Early Warning in the Lower Tana River Basin, Kenya.

Study area.

Purpose. To visualise relative desert locust risk by year, explore environmental conditions, summarise the distribution of risk and support early warning and preparedness planning by county officers, researchers and decision-makers.

Environmental data. Rainfall and precipitation concentration (CHIRPS), air temperature, humidity and soil moisture (ERA5-Land), vegetation indices and land surface temperature (Landsat 8/9), terrain (SRTM, MERIT Hydro), soil texture (OpenLandMap) and land cover (ESA WorldCover). Wind speed and wind direction are not used.

Modelling approach. Random Forest and XGBoost trained on desert locust occurrence and background points; an ensemble score is classified into Very Low, Low, Moderate, High and Very High.

Climate-informed risk projections (exploratory). A separate climate-only model (Random Forest and XGBoost, trained on 1995–2014 average climate at the same locust records) is applied to CMIP6 projections for 2025–2032, 2033–2040 and 2025–2040 under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5, using the delta method. These maps show projected climatic suitability, not forecasts of locust outbreaks, and are compared only with the same model's 1995–2014 baseline, not with the annual risk maps.

Climate outlook. Projected changes in rainfall, temperature, humidity and rainfall concentration come from NASA NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 (bias-corrected, downscaled CMIP6 models, about 27 km) under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5, compared with 1995–2014. They describe possible future climate conditions, not future locust risk maps.

Limitations. Classes show relative environmental suitability, not infestation probability. Models learn from 2020 records, so other years are compared with those conditions. Annual layers do not capture short-term swarm movement or control operations. Some environmental layers may not cover the full basin.

Intended use. Risk visualisation, early warning and preparedness planning alongside field surveillance, not instead of it.

How to read these maps. They show relative environmental suitability for the desert locust, estimated by statistical models. They are not forecasts, do not confirm locust presence and do not replace field surveillance.
